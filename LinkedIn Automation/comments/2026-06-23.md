Barrel files are the one that gets me every time. I've trimmed imports down to named exports, flipped on "sideEffects": false, and the bundle analyser still showed a whole icon library because one index.ts re-exported everything. Tree shaking only removes what the bundler can prove is unreachable. One side effect or a CJS (CommonJS, the older require/module.exports format) hop in the chain and the whole subgraph stays. The fix that actually moved the needle for me was importing from deep paths and auditing barrel re-exports, not swapping lodash for lodash-es and calling it done.

## Comment 2

Target: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/frontend-javascript-asyncjavascript-share-7474764622835806208-aVXL/

Promise.all is the trap here. It fires every request at once with no concurrency cap, so part 1 looks fine until the API rate-limits you or the tab runs out of memory. Part 2 usually wants a worker pool: N in flight, queue the rest, collect results in order. Part 3 adds priority, so a heap or separate high/low queues beat a flat queue. I'd also isolate errors per task instead of letting one rejection kill the whole batch. That's the gap between "I know async" and "I'd ship this in production."
