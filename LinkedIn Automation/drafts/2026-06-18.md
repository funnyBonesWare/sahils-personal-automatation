𝗬𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗱𝗲𝗺𝗼 𝗮𝘀𝘀𝘂𝗺𝗲𝘀 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗴𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝗿𝗶𝗴𝗵𝘁. 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗮𝘀𝘀𝘂𝗺𝗲𝘀 𝘀𝗼𝗺𝗲𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘄𝗶𝗹𝗹 𝗯𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗸.

Early in my career I thought "done" meant the feature worked in the demo. Green checkmark, happy path, stakeholders nodding. Then real users showed up. Duplicate clicks. Slow networks. Data that didn't match the mock. The UI looked fine. The system underneath wasn't built for any of it.

That gap never went away. AI just made it louder. You can spin up a working prototype in an afternoon now. Impressive in a meeting. Still not a product.

1️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗱𝗲𝗺𝗼𝘀 𝗼𝗽𝘁𝗶𝗺𝗶𝘇𝗲 𝗳𝗼𝗿

Speed. Presentation. Proving the idea is possible. Happy-path data. One user at a time. That's a valid goal. You need it to get buy-in. But demo code answers a different question than production code. Confusing the two is how teams ship something that collapses the first Monday after launch.

2️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗼𝗽𝘁𝗶𝗺𝗶𝘇𝗲𝘀 𝗳𝗼𝗿

Recovery. Retries. Monitoring. The weird edge case nobody planned. What happens when the API is slow, the user double-clicks, or the retry arrives out of order. Users never notice this layer when it works. They definitely notice when it's missing.

3️⃣ 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗾𝘂𝗲𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝘀𝗵𝗶𝗳𝘁 𝗜 𝘄𝗶𝘀𝗵 𝗜'𝗱 𝗺𝗮𝗱𝗲 𝘀𝗼𝗼𝗻𝗲𝗿

Junior me asked: does it work? Senior me asks: what happens when it fails? Not pessimism. Just honesty. Eventually something breaks. The work after the feature "works" is often observability, rollback paths, and idempotency. The stuff that never makes a good screenshot.

AI didn't remove that work. It just moved the demo closer to free. The production gap is still yours.

When did you first learn the demo and the product were two different jobs?
